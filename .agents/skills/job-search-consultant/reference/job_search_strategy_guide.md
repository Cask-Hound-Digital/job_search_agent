# Job Search Strategy Guide (By Executive Resume Strategists)

## Executive Summary
Job searching without a defined strategy leads to burnout, confusion, and low response rates. This guide details a structured, step-by-step job search framework built on goal setting, market research, employer alignment, and trust-earning networking.

---

## Chapter 1: Defining Your Goals

### The Dual Goal Approach
A successful job search balances **Short-Term Goals** (your immediate next move) with **Long-Term Career Goals** (your ultimate career vision).

### Goal Definition Matrix
Fill out the following parameters to gain total clarity on your target:

| Parameter | Long-Term Goal (Ultimate Role) | Short-Term Goal (Next Job) |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifestyle** | Desired work/life balance, remote vs. on-site, travel expectations | Immediate personal & schedule needs |
| **Industry** | Target sectors (e.g., Tech, Non-profit, Healthcare) | Target sectors for your next move |
| **Job Title / Duties** | Dream position title & high-level responsibilities | Practical titles & core responsibilities |
| **Employer Type** | Organization size, culture, prestige level | Immediate company targets/parameters |
| **Salary** | Long-term target compensation | Minimum acceptable floor & target salary |
| **Working Environment** | Physical & cultural setting | Office, hybrid, or work-from-home |
| **Other Elements** | Special projects, growth trajectory | Key skills needed to build towards long-term goal |

### Rule of Focus
- Limit your focus to **1 or 2 target job goals** at a time. Trying to pursue 3+ directions simultaneously dilutes focus, increases stress, and confuses hiring managers.

## Chapter 2: Uncovering Employer Requirements

### The 3 Core Requirements for Landing Any Job
1. **Uncover the Employer's Requirements**: Understand what the hiring manager needs.
2. **Show You Meet or Exceed Requirements**: Prove alignment across resume, cover letter, LinkedIn, and interviews.
3. **Earn Decision Maker Trust**: Reduce hiring risk through warm connections and referrals.

### Dissecting the Job Posting Formula
$$\text{If Employer Requirements} = \text{Your Demonstrated Experience} \implies \text{You Qualify for Interview}$$

#### Step-by-Step Posting Dissection:
1. Read the job description carefully.
2. Extract **Hard Skills & Experience**: Job-related skills, specific achievements sought, technology/software, education, certifications.
3. Extract **Soft Skills & Behaviors**: Professional traits, working environment descriptors (e.g., fast-paced, collaborative, customer-obsessed).
4. Categorize skills into **3-Star** (top "wow" selling points), **2-Star** (secondary core skills), and **1-Star** (supporting traits/skills).

## Chapter 3: Earning Decision Maker Trust (The 3TC System)

Hiring is a high-risk decision. Managers fear bad hires (toxic team dynamic, underperformance, hand-holding needs). They naturally hire from **The 3 Trustworthy Categories (3TC)**:
1. **Category 1**: Internal Candidates.
2. **Category 2**: People They Know From Elsewhere.
3. **Category 3**: Candidates Recommended by Someone They Trust.

### Outreach & Networking Scripts

#### Script 1: Reaching Out to a Hiring Manager You Already Know
> *"Hi [Name], I hope you’re doing well! I am reaching out because I saw that you’re hiring for a [Job Title] position at [Company Name]. Given my background in [industry] and [type of work], I found this job extremely intriguing and would love the chance to work with you. Before I apply, I would love to chat with you a bit about the role to get a better understanding of the scope of responsibilities and work environment. Would you be available for a quick chat in the next few days? Thanks! [Your Name]"*

#### Script 2: Asking a Mutual Contact for an Introduction
> *"Hi [Name], I hope you’re doing well! I am reaching out to see if you know [Hiring Manager’s Name] well enough to introduce me? There is a job opening on their team and I’d love the chance to connect with them before I apply in order to get a better idea of what the role entails. Do you think you’d be able to introduce me via email? Thanks!"*

#### Script 3: Instant Response to Email Introduction (Reply All within minutes)
> *"Thank you so much for the introduction, [Contact’s Name]! [Hiring Manager’s Name], it is great to virtually meet you! I wanted to connect because I saw that you’re hiring for a [Job Title] position at [Company Name]. Given my background in [industry] and [type of work], I found this job extremely intriguing. If you have a chance, I would love to chat with you a bit about the role. Would you be available for a quick chat in the next few days? Thanks! [Your Name]"*

#### Script 4: Direct Cold Outreach to Hiring Manager
> *"Hi [Hiring Manager’s Name], My name is [Your Name] and I’m reaching out because I was incredibly excited to read about the [Job Title] position at [Company Name]. Given my background in [industry], I found this job extremely intriguing. If you have a chance, I would love to chat with you a bit about the role to get a better understanding of the scope of responsibilities. Would you be available for a quick chat in the next few days? If not, I completely understand and am happy to connect through the formal application process. Thanks so much! [Your Name]"*

#### Script 5: Asking for a Recommendation ("Putting in a Good Word")
> *"Hi [Name], Thank you again for introducing me to [Hiring Manager’s Name]! We had a really great conversation about the role. I just submitted my application and am hoping to hear back about an interview. If it’s not too much trouble, I know it would go a long way if you were willing to share with [Hiring Manager’s Name] a bit about our experiences working together. I know they trust you so your opinion carries a lot of weight. Thanks so much! [Your Name]"*

## Chapter 4: Informational Interviews Framework

### The 3 Goals of a Networking Call
1. **Learn**: Ask about career paths, job responsibilities, company culture, and industry trends.
2. **Make a Friend**: Listen actively, let them talk about themselves, build genuine rapport, do not aggressively self-pitch.
3. **Get Help / Next Steps**: Ask for introductions to others, portfolio feedback, or leads on who is hiring.

### Top Informational Interview Questions (Prepare 10, Ask 4-6)
1. *Can you walk me through your career history and how you ended up at [Company Name]?*
2. *What are you responsible for in your role today?*
3. *What is it like to work at [Company Name]?*
4. *What do you like (and dislike) about working in [Industry]?*
5. *What advice would you share with a younger version of yourself?*
6. *What training, certifications, or experience would you recommend I add to my resume?*

## Chapter 5: Application Strategy & Execution

### Quality over Quantity ("Anti-Spray and Pray")
- **Avoid "Spray & Pray"**: Applying to 20-30+ jobs per day leads to low response rates and burnout.
- **Recommended Cadence**: **5-10 high-quality, fully customized applications per week** paired with strategic 3TC networking.

### Application Tracking System (ATS) Sheet Columns
Track every job application with these key fields:
- Company Name & Job Title
- Date Applied & Link to Posting (Copy/paste job description text into a doc in case posting is taken down)
- Hiring Manager Contact Identified? (Yes/No)
- Outreach Sent / Informational Chat Held? (Yes/No)
- Recommendation Received? (Yes/No)
- Interview Status & Outcome Notes
